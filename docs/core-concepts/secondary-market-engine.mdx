Once a market graduates — or when an existing Monad coin is listed — it moves off the internal virtual curve onto the **secondary engine**.

Instead of trading against a standalone virtual pool, the perp now prices off real spot liquidity on Uniswap, backed by the **Counterflow Vault (`cfUSDC`)**.

---

## How pricing works

On the secondary engine, fills are based on three things:

### 1. The on-chain oracle
- **Uniswap v4 pools (graduated markets):** The engine tracks a smoothed moving average of the pool price. Samples are taken once a second and capped so a single quick swap can't spike the oracle.
- **Uniswap v3 pools (listed tokens):** Uses Uniswap's built-in 5-minute TWAP. If a token is paired with WMON, the price converts to USD using the main WMON/USDC pool.

### 2. "Worst-of" fill protection
To stop traders from front-running a lagging oracle or a moved spot pool, takers always fill at the **worse of the oracle price and the live pool price**:
- **Longs (buys):** Fill at whichever price is higher.
- **Shorts (sells):** Fill at whichever price is lower.

This means you can't buy at an old low oracle price while the real pool is already higher, and you can't dump at a high oracle price while the real pool is lower.

### 3. Skew premium and spread
When a market gets lopsided (e.g. way more longs than shorts), the crowded side pays a small premium. The premium scales with the order size and is path-averaged, so splitting an order into small chunks costs the exact same as one big order. Every trade also includes a baseline 10 bps half-spread.

---

## Leverage and limits on secondary

- **Opening leverage:** Starts at 15x on day one of graduation, then steps up by 5x each day until reaching 40x.
- **Open interest caps:** Scaled directly to how much USDC depth the spot pool actually has.
- **Single-wallet limit:** No single trader can hold more than 25% of the market's total capacity.

---

## Backed by Counterflow

Graduated markets are backed by Counterflow, Priest's community liquidity vault. If a market runs into a temporary deficit paying out winning traders, Counterflow steps in to cover the difference. In exchange, Counterflow gets 30% of the market's trading fees and keeps the surplus when traders lose.
