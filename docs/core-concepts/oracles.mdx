Every perpetual market needs a reference price to calculate funding rates and decide when a position is up for liquidation. Here is how Priest.trade gets its prices on-chain.

---

## 1. Primary markets (vAMM)

When a coin first launches, there is no external spot pool yet. The engine calculates two internal prices:

- **Mark Price:** The current instantaneous price calculated directly from the virtual curve ($x \cdot y = k$). Fills happen here.
- **Index Price:** An on-chain Exponential Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) of recent fills. This filters out single-trade wicks and is used to compute funding rates and liquidation triggers.

---

## 2. Secondary markets (Uniswap v4 & v3)

Once a market graduates or an existing coin is listed, it references real spot pools on Monad:

- **Uniswap v4 (graduated coins):** The engine tracks a smoothed moving average of the pool price. Samples are taken once per second and clamped so sudden spikes can't spoof the price.
- **Uniswap v3 (listed coins):** Uses Uniswap's built-in 5-minute time-weighted average (TWAP).
- **MON pairs:** If a coin is paired with WMON instead of USDC, the price converts to USD using the main WMON/USDC pool TWAP.

---

## 3. "Worst-of" fill protection

To make sure traders can't exploit small delays between a fast spot swap and a slower moving average, executions use **worst-of pricing**:

- **Buying (Long):** Fills at whichever is higher between the live spot price and the oracle average.
- **Selling (Short):** Fills at whichever is lower between the live spot price and the oracle average.

This stops people from buying at an artificially low lagging price when the spot pool is already pumping, or shorting at a high lagging price when the pool is dumping.

---

## 4. Liquidations stay on the oracle

While fills use the worst-of rule, **liquidations rely on the smoothed oracle average**. If someone manipulates the spot pool for a single block, your position will not be liquidated. The smoothed average ignores the wick, protecting honest traders.
